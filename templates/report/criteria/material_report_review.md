# Supplied Material Review

Check that the report answers the user's question using the supplied material.
Preserve the distinction between source assertions, user-declared conditions,
verified computation and unknowns. Check important values, units, missing data,
figures and source disagreements against the evidence provided.

Do not require an experiment, baseline, hypothesis, failed objective, novelty or
continuation decision where none exists. Recommendations must not become claims
of completed or authorized work. Do not require citations for user material;
check bibliographic citations when present and reject invented metadata.

Check reader-facing organization: findings have one main home, interpretation
explains rather than repeats, limitations are relevant, and the conclusion is
short and supported. Distinguish factual defects from optional style advice.
